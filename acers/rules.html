Race Rules & Scoring

1. Series format & starts

Autumn 2026 is a six-race series held on Monday evenings at 1825hrs. Race 1 is an Individual Time Trial / Performance Deployment Race (PDR) using a single-pen mass start. It is intended to benchmark performance and help riders establish an accurate Zwift Racing Score.

The remaining rounds use the normal Zwift pen structure, with a one-minute delay between pens. The current course, distance, elevation, prime and power-up information is published on the live Schedule page.

Participation in the Race 1 PDR is not mandatory to compete in the rest of the series.
2. Competition eligibility

Riders declare either Open or Female competition eligibility at registration. The provisional instruction sets out the eligibility criteria in full.

Female riders enter the ZRS pen appropriate to their current score, then are ranked and scored within the Female competition by category and time.

For questions about eligibility, use the full instruction or raise the matter through the ACeRS committee/Corps representative rather than relying only on this summary.

3. Zwift Racing Score categories

Autumn 2026 uses Zwift Racing Score (ZRS) for pen/category placement. Riders cannot enter a pen lower than the category available to them in Zwift.

ZRS can change during the series. If promoted, a rider must race in the higher category. If a lower category later becomes available, the rider may choose to race there. Riders should notify the ACeRS committee when their category changes.

4. Individual competitions & scoring

There are four individual classifications per category:

  • Points Competition — finishing-position points plus Sprint/KQOM points. Only a rider's best three race scores count to the final Points standing.
  • General Classification (GC) — cumulative race time. Missing-race/category-change adjustments are applied in accordance with the full rules.
  • Sprint Classification — fastest-time (FTS) ranking on designated short sprint primes.
  • KQOM Classification — fastest-time ranking on designated climbing primes.

5. Corps competition

The Corps competition uses the best seven points scores across the competitions for each stage, as detailed in the provisional instruction. Riders should keep their Corps/unit affiliation up to date so points are attributed correctly.

6. Membership, registration & Verified status

Riders must be paid-up Army Cycling Union members for points and race times to count towards official individual or Corps competitions. Riders who are not verified may still ride, but their scores do not count.

Registration is required once for the series. If a rider's Corps, category, weight or other registration details change, they should resubmit the registration form.

7. Results, disputes & appeals

ZwiftPower results published shortly after the race are provisional and unfiltered. Official ACeRS results are intended to be published on this site within 24 hours after membership and race-processing checks.

Result disputes should be raised through the appropriate Corps Representative within 24 hours of the official results being released.

8. Equipment & heart-rate requirements

A heart-rate monitor is compulsory for racing. The rules recognise that equipment failures can happen, but repeated/more significant HRM failures can lead to relegation or disqualification depending on category and circumstances.

ZPower remains permitted to keep the series accessible, but sanctions apply where its use could create an unfair advantage. Open Cat A/B riders using ZPower are not placed; Cat C/D Open and Female riders may be relegated to last place in accordance with the rules.

9. Fair sport, abnormal readings & verification

Riders must race honestly and keep Zwift height/weight information accurate. Exploiting software behaviour such as sticky watts or microbursts to gain an advantage can lead to race disqualification and, for repeated behaviour, removal from the series.

The committee may review rider profiles and request weight/height verification. A verification request is issued after a race and the evidence must be supplied before the next race under the full protocol. Results may be suspended while a performance is being verified.

Concerns about suspicious performance should be raised through a Corps representative or the official reporting route — not on social media.

10. Safety & duty requirements

Riders are responsible for following the safety requirements in the Administrative Instruction and Risk Assessment. Key requirements include:

  • Progressive pre-series training.
  • Completion of Module 1 Heat Illness Prevention training.
  • Chain-of-command permission where ACeRS forms part of a Unit Physical Training Programme.
  • Using equipment in accordance with manufacturer guidance.
  • Water, adequate ventilation and access to a cooling fan while racing.
  • Where possible, ensuring someone in the household knows the rider is racing.
  • Reading and understanding the published Risk Assessment before taking part.
